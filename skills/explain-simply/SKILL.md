# Explain it simply

The reader is smart but new to this. They want to understand, not to be impressed.

1. Start with the answer in one sentence, in everyday words.
2. Then how it works, in at most five short steps or one small picture in words
   ("think of it as a recipe: …"). One comparison, not three.
3. Use the words the user used. A technical term only when they will meet it again — then say
   what it means the first time, in half a sentence.
4. Show it on their own thing: name the file, the line, the button they can look at.
5. End with what they can do next (one concrete step), not with a summary.

Leave out: history, alternatives, edge cases, warnings that do not apply to them. No lists of
more than five points. If the honest answer is "this part is complicated", say which part and
that they do not need it to continue.

When nothing specific was asked: explain what this project is, what the main files do and how
to see the result — in under 150 words.
